Split — a personal training instrument. Data: Intervals.icu (Garmin FR255), scored by the daily coaching routine.

Methods & definitions

Everything here is computed in the browser from static JSON: the coaching log, an athlete reference, daily wellness rows, and one full-resolution stream file per training day.

Day score

0–100, a probability-change reading: did the day move the odds of the 1:20 dream goal up (>60), hold them (~60), or lower them (<60)? Scored independently against 1:20 and against the committed 1:24–1:27 line — the two can diverge, and the gap between them is itself the signal. It rewards goal-fit, not effort: an easy run done genuinely easy scores high; intensity on an easy day scores low. Most days sit 50–75; the extremes are reserved for real probability events.

The call, and the raced-today odds

The call is the projected half time at current fitness — threshold estimate plus 5K-shape conversion, minus an endurance discount, cross-checked against the Nov 2025 benchmark (1:33:42 at CTL 25). It moves at most ~30 s/day without a race or a missed week. The raced-today probability is Φ((goal − projection) / σ) with σ ≈ 3% — the chance of hitting the goal if the race were today. Both goals round to ~0% through base phase by design; the live signal is the projection falling toward the goal lines, which is why this dashboard leads with the gap in minutes rather than a flat-zero percentage.

Pace & GAP

Pace comes from the device velocity stream (7 s smoothing for display, raw for computation). GAP (grade-adjusted pace) applies a Minetti metabolic-cost grade model, calibrated per activity to Intervals.icu's own GAP figure so splits stay consistent with the activity total. Kilometre splits are computed on moving time, so split pace and split GAP share one clock.

Session type & structure

Every run is classified automatically — no watch laps required. The primary source is Intervals.icu's server-side auto-detected work/recovery segments (computed on full-resolution data and stored in the day file); each segment is judged work-or-float against that session's own grade-adjusted speed spread, so a progression stays whole and a rep set separates. A velocity-stream detector is the fallback. Workouts show a warmup / reps / recoveries / cooldown table with the work segments shaded on the pace chart, and skip kilometre splits; easy, steady and long runs show splits instead. Raw autolaps are never displayed — on a steady run they are just kilometre beeps.

Decoupling (Pw:Hr) and efficiency

Moving time is split in half; EF₁/EF₂ = mean GAP speed ÷ mean heart rate per half; decoupling = (EF₁−EF₂)/EF₁. Positive means the same speed cost more heart rate late — aerobic drift. It is a clean base signal only on steady efforts of ~25 min or more; on a progression the negative split dominates and the reading is flagged accordingly. EF is metres travelled per heartbeat at GAP speed: rising EF at the same heart rate is the base building.

Easy purity & intensity mix

Easy purity is the share of a run's time in HR zones 1–2. The weekly mix buckets each activity by Intervals' intensity (GAP speed ÷ threshold speed): easy <80%, steady 80–88%, threshold 88–96%, hard ≥96%; unrated fragments count as easy. For this athlete these two are the headline metrics — the documented failure mode is intensity on days that were meant to be easy.

Heart-rate zones, cadence, power

Z1≤145 · Z2≤153 · Z3≤162 · Z4≤171 · Z5≤176 · Z6≤181 · Z7≤190 (LTHR 172, max 190). Zone times use Intervals' computation where present, else the HR stream. The device records cadence per leg (~86); it is doubled for display as steps/min. Power is native Garmin wrist running power; NP is a 30 s rolling average, then the fourth root of the mean fourth power.

Streams & sampling

Full 1-second device streams, no downsampling. Aggregates and workout-segment boundaries come from Intervals.icu directly. GPS traces are never published.

Colour & accessibility

Series colours are steps from a documented palette, assigned per entity in a fixed order and never cycled. The set was validated against this page's chart surface for lightness band, chroma floor, colourblind separation (worst adjacent ΔE 8.4 under protan/deutan simulation), normal-vision separation (19.3) and ≥3:1 contrast. No chart uses two y-axes. Every chart has a legend when it plots more than one series, and a data button that reveals the underlying table.
