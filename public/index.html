How everything is calculated

Everything on this page is computed in the browser from static files: the coaching log, an athlete reference, daily wellness rows, and one full-resolution stream file per training day.

Day score

A 0–100 reading of whether the day moved the odds of the 1:20 goal up (above 60), held them (around 60), or lowered them (below 60). It is scored separately against 1:20 and against the committed 1:24–1:27, and the two can move in opposite directions — that gap is itself a signal. It rewards fit-to-purpose, not effort: an easy run done genuinely easy scores high, and intensity on a day meant to be easy scores low. Most days land between 50 and 75.

The projected time

The half-marathon time current fitness supports — a threshold estimate and 5K-shape conversion, less an endurance discount, cross-checked against the November 2025 time trial (1:33:42 at CTL 25). It moves at most about 30 seconds a day without a race or a missed week. The chance of actually hitting each goal today is Φ((goal − projection) / σ) with σ ≈ 3%, which rounds to zero all through base phase — so this page shows the gap in minutes instead, which is the number that actually changes.

Pace and grade-adjusted pace

Pace comes from the device velocity stream, smoothed over 7 seconds for display and left raw for calculation. Grade-adjusted pace applies a Minetti metabolic-cost model, calibrated per activity to Intervals.icu's own figure so the splits reconcile with the activity total. Kilometre splits use moving time, so split pace and split GAP share one clock.

Session type, and reading a workout

Each run is classified automatically, with no watch laps required: the work and recovery segments Intervals.icu detects server-side are judged against that session's own grade-adjusted speed spread, gated first on absolute intensity so a hilly easy run is never mistaken for a workout. Workouts show warmup, reps, recoveries and cooldown, with the work shaded on the pace chart; easy and long runs show kilometre splits instead. Every run also gets a deeper read the averages hide — zone distribution, peak heart rate as a share of maximum, how much of the session was standing rest, whether heart rate recovered between reps, and how set pace compares both to threshold and to recent race pace.

Drift and efficiency

Moving time is halved; efficiency for each half is mean grade-adjusted speed divided by mean heart rate, and decoupling is the fall between them. Positive means the same speed cost more heart rate later — aerobic drift. It only means something on steady efforts of 25 minutes or more; on a progression the negative split dominates and it is flagged as such. Efficiency factor is metres travelled per heartbeat, and rising efficiency at the same heart rate is the base building.

Easy share and the intensity mix

Easy share is the portion of weekly miles run below 80% of threshold; within a single run, easy purity is time spent in heart-rate zones 1–2. The weekly mix buckets each run as easy (under 80%), steady (80–88%), threshold (88–96%) or hard (96% and above). These are the headline numbers here because the documented failure mode is intensity on days that were meant to be easy.

Zones, cadence, power

Z1 up to 145, Z2 to 153, Z3 to 162, Z4 to 171, Z5 to 176, Z6 to 181, Z7 to 190, from a threshold heart rate of 172 and a maximum of 190. The watch records cadence per leg, so it is doubled to steps per minute. Power is Garmin's native wrist running power; normalised power is a 30-second rolling average raised to the fourth power, averaged, then rooted.

Streams and colour

Full one-second streams, never downsampled; aggregates and segment boundaries come from Intervals.icu directly; GPS traces are never published. Series colours are steps from a documented palette, assigned per measure in a fixed order and validated for colourblind separation and contrast against this page's own background. No chart uses two vertical axes, every chart with more than one line has a legend, and each has a button that reveals the numbers as a table.
